# System Requirements

## Purpose

This document defines the functional and non-functional requirements for the Technology Governance Assessment and Prioritization process.

The requirements describe what the process needs to accomplish and the characteristics it should have to support small and growing organizations in translating existing technology governance knowledge into organization-specific priorities.

## Functional Requirements

### FR1: Provide Literature-Based Governance Practices

The solution shall provide the eight core literature sources and the technology governance practices identified from those sources.

### FR2: Document Existing Practices

The solution shall allow the organization to document its existing technology governance practices, including informal practices where applicable.

### FR3: Compare Existing and Literature-Based Practices

The solution shall allow the organization's existing practices to be compared with the technology governance practices identified from the literature.

### FR4: Assess Practices Against Organizational Factors

The solution shall allow practices to be assessed against organizational factors relevant to the organization's circumstances.

### FR5: Apply Organizational Scoring Criteria

The solution shall allow organizational weights or point values to be applied to the assessment criteria and shall produce a score for each assessed practice.

### FR6: Assign Priority

The solution shall assign a priority category to each assessed practice according to organizational scoring thresholds.

### FR7: Support Practice Selection

The solution shall support the selection of literature-based practices for inclusion in the organization's practice list based on assessment results and priority.

### FR8: Assess Feasibility

The solution shall allow the organization to assess whether selected practices can currently be implemented based on available resources and organizational constraints.

### FR9: Document Assessment and Decision Results

The solution shall allow assessment results, scores, priorities, feasibility findings, and decisions to be documented, including reasons for inclusion, exclusion, or deprioritization.

### FR10: Produce an Implementation Roadmap

The solution shall produce an implementation roadmap containing:

- Rank
- Practice
- Description

### FR11: Support Implementation Evaluation

The solution shall support evaluation of implemented practices, including whether the practice was implemented, what issues occurred, why issues occurred, whether additional organizational support could have helped, and what improvements are needed.

### FR12: Support Reassessment and Adaptation

The solution shall support reassessment of practices, priorities, feasibility decisions, and the implementation roadmap when appropriate or when significant organizational circumstances change.

## Non-Functional Requirements

### NFR1: Usability

The process should be understandable and practical for small and growing organizations and should not require unnecessary complexity to apply.

### NFR2: Traceability

The solution should allow decisions to be traced from the governance practice through assessment, scoring, priority, feasibility, and roadmap decisions.

### NFR3: Security

Assessment information should be appropriately protected and accessible only to authorized individuals. The specific security controls may depend on the format used to implement the process.

### NFR4: Scalability

The process should remain workable as an organization grows and as additional practices, participants, or assessment information are added.

### NFR5: Maintainability

The process and its records should be capable of being updated as organizational circumstances, practices, responsibilities, or assessment criteria change.

## Requirements Boundary

The requirements describe the capabilities and characteristics of the proposed assessment and prioritization process. They do not assume that the final solution must be implemented as a conventional software application.

The process may be implemented using structured documents, spreadsheets, a lightweight prototype, software, or another appropriate format. If implemented using software, technical requirements such as authentication, authorization, and data protection can be specified as part of that implementation.
